An animated travel map works because it shows the part a photo album usually
misses: movement. A camera roll can show Paris, Lyon, and Istanbul, but it
rarely shows the feeling of going from one place to the next.

Cinemaly is built around that missing layer. You add stops in order, attach the
photos and notes that belong to each place, and the trip becomes a cinematic map
story instead of another grid of thumbnails.

<Callout>
  The best animated travel maps are not just pretty routes. They preserve
  sequence, geography, and memory at the same time.
</Callout>

## What You Need Before You Start

You do not need a full video editor or a GPS tracker. You need three simple
ingredients:

- The stops you visited, in order
- The photos that actually represent those stops
- A few short notes that explain what each place felt like

That structure is enough for Cinemaly to create a map story that feels like a
trip rather than a slideshow.

## How to Build the Map

<Step num="01" title="Choose the stops that define the route">
  Start with the cities, towns, viewpoints, or regions that shaped the journey.
  Keep them in the order you traveled. The order matters because the map will
  use it to create the sense of motion.
</Step>

<Step num="02" title="Attach photos where they belong">
  Pick photos for each stop, not just the most polished photos overall. A
  slightly imperfect image from the right place often tells the story better
  than a perfect image with no context.
</Step>

<Step num="03" title="Add short notes">
  Write one or two lines for each location. Notes do not need to be diary
  entries. They just need to help the viewer understand why this stop mattered.
</Step>

<Step num="04" title="Export the capsule">
  On mobile, Cinemaly exports a `.cnmly` travel capsule you can keep or share.
  The web version can still compile a browser-based `.html` capsule.
</Step>

## Privacy Notes

Cinemaly does not upload your capsules, photos, notes, or videos to a Cinemaly
server. Your personal travel content is created and kept on your device. Place
search may send the location query to Nominatim/OpenStreetMap.
